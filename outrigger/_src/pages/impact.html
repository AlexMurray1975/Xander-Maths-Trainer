Impact is measured against a published framework of seven themes and fourteen indicators, applied at investment and monitored through the life of each holding. The methodology and its sources are set out below.

How these targets were set

The targets were derived from the fund’s expected size, its expected capital allocation across the six investment sectors, published literature, and the team’s experience of comparable vehicles — principally the Althelia / Mirova Sustainable Ocean Fund. Targets have been set only for those indicators where an estimate can reasonably be made; the remainder of the indicator set will be targeted as projects enter the portfolio.

A further target — 15 enterprises with enhanced resilience — sits alongside the four above.

What they are, and are not

These are objectives, not forecasts or guarantees. They are portfolio-level aims measured against the framework below, and are not a projection of any individual investment. They will be revised as the portfolio develops, so that they remain both ambitious and a true reflection of what the fund actually holds.

The fund contributes directly to seven of the UN Sustainable Development Goals: no poverty (1), gender equality (5), clean water and sanitation (6), reduced inequalities (10), climate action (13), life below water (14) and life on land (15). Its primary contribution is to SDG 14, the least funded of the seventeen goals.

Standards

The commitments behind the numbers

  • A SIDS-tailored impact framework

    Seven impact themes with dedicated indicators for reporting, developed from the fund’s own theory of change rather than borrowed from a mainland template.

  • IFC performance standards

    All investments are required to uphold sustainability and ESG standards including the IFC performance standards, alongside achieving best-practice certifications where relevant.

  • SFDR Article 8

    The fund is classified under Article 8 of the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation. It contributes to the Sustainable Development Goals and to Global Biodiversity Framework targets. The extent of any sustainable-investment commitment under Article 2(17) SFDR is set out in the fund’s pre-contractual disclosures.

  • A dedicated ESG team

    Separate project monitoring and reporting of ESG progress, independent of the deal team.

  • UNEP Sustainable Blue Economy Finance Principles

    Aligned with the United Nations Environment Programme’s principles for financing a sustainable blue economy.

  • An environmental and social management system

    ESG and impact risk is mitigated by a detailed ESMS applied across the portfolio.

Impact themes

Seven themes, with climate adaptation running through all of them

Climate adaptation is a cross-cutting theme. Improvements in biodiversity and ecosystems, alongside enhanced environmental and economic resilience, support SIDS communities and environments to adapt to the existing and expected impacts of climate change.

Cross-cutting. The number of people whose climate resilience measurably improves.

Theme 03

Biodiversity

Ecosystem enhancement and species protection across seascapes and landscapes.

Theme 04

Environmental resilience

Pollution reduction and blue infrastructure that protects coastlines from extreme weather.

Theme 05

Economic resilience

Resilient enterprises, certified sustainable production, import substitution and investment brought onto the island.

Theme 06

Livelihoods

Direct jobs and wider livelihoods created or supported, including cooperative membership.

Theme 07

Inclusion

Gender equity and the inclusion of all groups relevant to the local context.

Two commitments in detail

Gender equity and poverty, stated as obligations rather than aspirations

Gender equity

Across Small Island Developing States, women’s participation in the labour force varies widely by region, but in most cases remains below men’s. Outrigger treats the identification of project-level barriers to women’s professional progression as part of the investment and grant process, not as reporting after the fact.

Two requirements apply across the portfolio. All projects must operate inclusively, with no discrimination against women in any of their activities. And all grantees must develop and implement a policy prohibiting gender-based violence and harassment.

Contributing to SDG 5.1 — ending discrimination against women and girls — and SDG 5.2 — eliminating violence against women and girls.

Poverty

Most Small Island Developing States are classified as middle income; one is classified as low income and seven as Least Developed. Those classifications understate the position. Research by ODI finds that the cumulative effect of the climate shocks these states face repeatedly erodes living standards and limits people’s capacity to absorb the next one — and that a third of the population of SIDS is poor, or vulnerable to falling into poverty.

The Technical Assistance Facility takes a multidimensional approach to poverty. Rather than applying a single threshold across very different island economies, each project defines the measure appropriate to its own context and target beneficiaries.

Contributing to SDG 1.5 — building the resilience of the poor and those in vulnerable situations, and reducing their exposure to climate-related extreme events and other shocks, by 2030.
