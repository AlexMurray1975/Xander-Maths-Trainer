The Outrigger Technical Assistance Facility

Most island projects are not un-investable. They are un-ready. OTAF closes that distance — building the capacity, capability and ecosystem for blue economy SMEs across Small Island Developing States.

In brief

Designed in conjunction with the fund, and working in tandem with it

OTAF has been developed alongside the Outrigger Impact Fund and works with it to catalyse funding and investment in early-stage projects, helping develop social, economic and environmental resilience.

It is constituted separately, as a UK company limited by guarantee with its own Grant Committee of key donors and Outrigger management. It soft-launched in mid-2025 and is now supporting projects across the Caribbean, the Indian Ocean and the Pacific. The facility has received funding from USAID; from UK International Development, through the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs and the Ocean Risk and Resilience Action Alliance; and from the Global Environment Facility, in partnership with Conservation International. The Nordic Development Fund has also committed to the facility. Projects supported by OTAF may go on to seek investment from the fund.

What OTAF funds

Four workstreams

01

Investment readiness

Support for projects with strong investment potential that need targeted assistance to address the gaps preventing them from securing investment — whether those gaps are of maturity, financial, operational, impact-related or ESG-related.

02

Capacity building

Building capacity within SIDS: the technical capacity needed to develop Marine Spatial Plans and to develop and implement ocean stewardship policy, alongside expanding business capability within SMEs.

03

Ecosystem restoration & conservation

Restoration and conservation are vital to building environmental resilience to climate change. By improving ecosystem health, OTAF supports coastal livelihoods through fishing and eco-tourism, and strengthens natural coastal protection. The facility aims to enhance the protection and conservation of 10,000 hectares of coastal and marine areas.

04

Poverty alleviation

Strengthening SIDS economies drives resilience at two scales. At island scale, through businesses built on high environmental and social foundations with strategies for adapting to a changing climate. At individual scale, through access to climate-resilient jobs and livelihoods that bring regular income to poor and vulnerable communities.

Portfolio

Four grants, US$1 million, four island problems

Read the four case studies

Since the soft launch in mid-2025, the Grant Committee has approved four grants of US$250,000 each. Each addresses a problem that is specific, local and expensive to live with.

  • INVERSA — Belize

    Invasive species. Establishing a market for invasive lionfish, producing leather for the fashion industry and meat for pet food, in waters where native prey biomass has collapsed.

  • SarGas — Grenada

    Harmful algal blooms. Using sargassum as feedstock for biogas production, generating electricity for the national grid and organic fertiliser as a by-product.

  • ABALOBI — Atlantic & Indian Ocean

    Data-deficient fisheries. Extending a fisher-designed catch data platform to the Maldives, Seychelles, Mauritius, Comoros and Cabo Verde.

  • Positive Change for Marine Life — Solomon Islands

    Livelihoods under pressure. Building a community-led seaweed cooperative for fisher families in a province where traditional fishing and forestry no longer generate sufficient income.

How it got here

From first donor funds to a working grant portfolio, in twelve months

  1. May 2025

    Grant funds received from UK International Development to set up OTAF and support blue economy projects across SIDS.

  2. June 2025

    The Nordic Development Fund commits grant funds to the facility.

  3. May – September 2025

    Grant processes established and the first project reviews carried out.

  4. September 2025

    First projects presented to the Grant Committee, including INVERSA.

  5. December 2025

    Grant to SarGas approved, supporting the transformation of sargassum into biogas, electricity and organic fertiliser in Grenada.

  6. January 2026

    OTAF makes its first grant, to INVERSA, to establish a market for invasive lionfish in Belize.

  7. February 2026

    Grant funds received from the Global Environment Facility.

  8. March 2026

    Positive Change for Marine Life approved by the Grant Committee, to develop seaweed aquaculture in the Solomon Islands.

  9. May 2026

    The Grant Committee approves a grant to ABALOBI to extend its fisheries data platform to Atlantic and Indian Ocean island states.

How OTAF works

Early-stage blue economy projects and SMEs across Small Island Developing States, with strong potential to scale. The facility works across the whole of Outrigger’s eligible geography — all thirty-five Big Ocean States. Because it makes grants rather than investments, it can also support island communities and projects too small to be eligible for the fund.

Grants and repayable loans of $50,000 to $250,000, over a three-year term, funding feasibility studies, proof-of-concept work, and early analysis of the policy, operational or environmental questions that have to be answered before investment can proceed. OTAF also supports nature-based solutions — mangrove, seagrass and coral restoration projects that may develop into blue carbon or biodiversity credit initiatives — and organisational capacity, through business planning, ESG performance, policy development and impact management. Because it is a grant facility, it can back smaller island communities and projects that would never reach the scale the fund requires.

It can. Projects supported by OTAF may graduate to the Outrigger Impact Fund, but support is not a commitment to invest. Any investment remains subject to due diligence and Investment Committee approval on the same terms as any other opportunity.

OTAF is a UK company limited by guarantee, governed by a Grant Committee comprising key donors and Outrigger management. It is constituted separately from the Luxembourg fund vehicle, and its grant-making decisions are made independently of the fund’s Investment Committee.

Through donor commitments. OTAF has received funding from USAID; from UK International Development, through the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs and the Ocean Risk and Resilience Action Alliance; and from the Global Environment Facility, in partnership with Conservation International. The Nordic Development Fund has also committed to the facility. At least 70% of OTAF’s grants will support ODA-eligible countries. Outrigger continues to welcome donor partners ahead of the fund’s final close in 2027.
