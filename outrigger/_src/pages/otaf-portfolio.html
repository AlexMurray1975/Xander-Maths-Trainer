Four grants, four island problems

Since the facility soft-launched in mid-2025, OTAF has approved four grants totalling US$1 million. Each one takes a specific, local problem — an invasive fish, an algal bloom, a data gap, a collapsing livelihood — and tests whether a business can be built around solving it.

Alongside assessing the projects in its pipeline, Outrigger is building a portfolio management system that carries business performance, ESG and impact reporting across the life of each grant. The figures on this page are the outcomes each project is expected to deliver, agreed at the point of award. They are not results, and they are not yet verified. They will be reported against as each project runs.

A grant from the facility is not an investment, and it is not a commitment by Outrigger Impact Fund I to invest. Projects may go on to seek investment; any investment would be subject to due diligence and Investment Committee approval on the same terms as any other opportunity.

Grant 01 · Belize

Establishing a supply chain for invasive lionfish

Grantee: INVERSA
Awarded: January 2026 — the facility’s first grant
Amount: US$250,000

Lionfish are invasive across the Caribbean, where their numbers have expanded rapidly because they have no natural predators in the region.

Reducing lionfish populations can increase native prey fish biomass by more than 50%, while creating a new revenue stream for local fishers in an area where many fisheries are already depleted. The grant supports a year-long project to demonstrate the commercial potential of a lionfish supply chain — leather to the fashion industry, meat to the pet food industry.

Grant 02 · Grenada

Electricity from the biodigestion of sargassum

Grantee: SarGas Limited
Approved by the Grant Committee: December 2025
Amount: US$250,000

Sargassum blooms are causing serious problems across the Caribbean Sea, the Gulf of Mexico, the Atlantic and the west coast of Africa.

When sargassum washes ashore and decays it disrupts fishing, harms marine ecosystems, produces strong odours, releases methane and hydrogen sulphide, and affects the daily wellbeing of thousands of residents. SarGas uses a mixture of sargassum and other organic waste to produce biogas and organic fertiliser in Grenada, burning the biogas to generate electricity that enters the national grid. The grant supports the development of a commercial-scale pilot, SarGas One.

Grant 03 · Atlantic & Indian Ocean

Supporting small-scale fisheries through data tools

Grantee: ABALOBI
Approved by the Grant Committee: May 2026
Amount: US$250,000
Countries: Maldives, Seychelles, Mauritius, Comoros, Cabo Verde

ABALOBI is a hybrid social enterprise that helps small-scale fishing communities move towards verifiably legal, well-documented and fairly regulated fisheries, through data and financial inclusion.

Building on its work in South Africa, this grant takes ABALOBI’s MONITOR platform to Atlantic and Indian Ocean island states. MONITOR is not a standalone app but the data layer of a wider platform, through which fisher monitors and their organisations record community-generated catch data to support fisheries management and co-management. It is co-designed with fishers and configured to each local context — its species, gear types, vessels and languages — rather than applied as a fixed template. As partners establish reliable data systems, the same platform can later connect fisher organisations to traceability and trade modules, opening market access and financial inclusion over time.

Grant 04 · Solomon Islands

Seaweed farming for reef resilience and livelihoods

Grantee: Positive Change for Marine Life
Approved by the Grant Committee: March 2026
Amount: US$250,000

Western Province in the Solomon Islands is a global biodiversity hotspot, and is under growing pressure from climate change, overfishing and economic insecurity.

The project builds on existing seaweed farming activity, working with local communities to develop a resilient cooperative seaweed platform that offers alternative livelihoods to fisher families. By improving production systems linked to water quality and strengthening buyer pathways, it aims to help communities produce higher-quality Pacific product while laying the foundations for a coordinated, community-led cooperative. The grant supports the initial pilot and the technical, market and cooperative groundwork required to scale it.
