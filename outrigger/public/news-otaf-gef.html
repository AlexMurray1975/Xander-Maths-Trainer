Global Environment Facility commits $1.6 million to the Outrigger Technical Assistance Facility to strengthen blue economy investment in Small Island Developing States

  • US$1.6 million through the GEF’s Special Climate Change Fund, in partnership with Conservation International, running through 2030
  • The facility has also received commitments from UK International Development, the Nordic Development Fund and USAID
  • Aims to enhance the protection and conservation of 10,000 hectares of coastal and marine areas

Reproduced as issued on 21 April 2026.

The Outrigger Technical Assistance Facility (OTAF), part of Outrigger Impact, is proud to announce a new $1.6 million commitment, supporting investment readiness for blue economy initiatives in Small Island Developing States (SIDS). In partnership with Conservation International and supported by the Global Environment Facility (GEF), this catalytic funding through the GEF’s Special Climate Change Fund will run through 2030, and enable the OTAF to help high-potential blue economy projects overcome key barriers to securing investment.

The OTAF focuses on advancing “investment readiness”, providing targeted grant support to projects with strong commercial and impact potential that require assistance to address gaps preventing them from attracting capital. These gaps may be financial, operational, impact-related, or linked to Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) standards.

The facility has also received commitments from UK International Development, Nordic Development Fund and USAID.

Driving poverty alleviation and economic resilience

The OTAF’s work is grounded in strengthening economic resilience within SIDS. By supporting businesses built on strong environmental and social foundations, and equipped with strategies to adapt to a changing climate, the OTAF contributes to resilience at multiple scales.

At the island level, resilient businesses underpin stable and diversified economies. At the community level, climate-resilient jobs and sustainable livelihoods provide reliable incomes for poor and vulnerable populations, helping reduce poverty and enhance long-term economic security.

Restoring ecosystems, strengthening communities

Ecosystem restoration and conservation sit at the core of the OTAF’s impact strategy. Healthy coastal and marine ecosystems are essential for climate resilience, food security, and economic opportunity. By improving ecosystem health, the OTAF will, for example, support livelihoods in fisheries and eco-tourism while strengthening natural coastal protection against climate impacts.

The OTAF aims to enhance the protection and conservation of 10,000 hectares of coastal and marine areas, contributing meaningfully to conservation goals while directly benefiting island communities.

Building local capacity for lasting impact

Achieving long-term transformation requires strong local institutions and enterprises. The OTAF will invest in building capacity within SIDS, including strengthening technical expertise for the development of Marine Protection Areas and ocean stewardship policies, as well as expanding the operational and strategic capabilities of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).

Advancing gender equity

Gender equity is a core value of the OTAF. The Facility will support projects to enhance gender inclusion, addressing barriers to women entering or advancing within organisations. The OTAF will also ensure that risks related to gender-based violence and harassment (GBVH) are identified and proactively addressed, embedding inclusive and safe practices across supported initiatives.

Focused on a sustainable blue economy

The OTAF is an innovative blue economy solution that provides capital and capacity to early-stage projects across the following priority areas:

  • Ocean conservation, nature-based solutions and ecotourism
  • Sustainable seafood (fisheries, mariculture and aquaculture)
  • Circular economy solutions and blue technology innovation
  • Sustainable blue infrastructure
  • Ridge-to-reef solutions addressing land-based challenges that threaten our oceans

Experienced leadership and strong networks

OTAF is uniquely positioned to support early-stage businesses in transitioning from grant-funded pilots to investment-backed sustainable enterprises, leveraging extensive networks of funders and strategic partners.

With the support from Conservation International and the Global Environment Facility, the OTAF is accelerating the pipeline of investable, high-impact blue economy ventures across Small Island Developing States, strengthening economies, restoring ecosystems, and building a more resilient future for island communities worldwide.

The OTAF has already made two grant commitments in the Caribbean and one in the Pacific:

  • INVERSA manages harmful, invasive, non-native species to revive ecosystems and is launching a Lionfish management programme, initially in Belize. The company produces high-value leather from fish skin and collaborates with a partner organisation to process the meat into pet food products, thereby creating viable markets and supporting the development of lionfish fisheries.
  • SarGas Limited is building a pilot biodigestion facility in Grenada to convert sargassum seaweed and organic waste into renewable biogas and organic fertiliser. Sargassum blooms cause significant damage to tourism, fisheries and health across the Caribbean.
  • Positive Change for Marine Life is working with local communities in the Western Province of the Solomon Islands to establish a seaweed farming cooperative and produce high quality dried seaweed for the export market.

The Facility is actively seeking sustainable business plans and opportunities on islands with clear pathways to scale and the potential to graduate to incremental investment following investment readiness support. For more information or to submit an opportunity, please contact the Outrigger Technical Assistance Facility team at otaf@outriggerimpact.com.

About Outrigger Impact

Outrigger Impact is dedicated to building environmental and economic resilience and driving nature-positive outcomes across ocean states by catalysing the blue economy and enhancing the sustainability of ocean resources. By providing transitional finance through both grant and investment into the sustainable blue economy, the platform will support SIDS to improve the sustainability and resilience of their economies, strengthen the resilience of their coastal based communities to climate change, and to conserve and enhance the biodiversity and ecosystems within their coastal regions and Exclusive Economic Zones. The Outrigger Impact Platform encompasses the OTAF (launched in 2025) and the aligned Outrigger Impact Fund I (to be launched in 2026).

About Conservation International

Conservation International protects nature for the benefit of humanity. Through science, policy, fieldwork and finance, we spotlight and secure the most important places in nature for the climate, for biodiversity and for people. With offices in 30 countries and projects in more than 100 countries, Conservation International partners with governments, companies, civil society, Indigenous peoples and local communities to help people and nature thrive together.

About the Global Environment Facility

The Global Environment Facility (GEF) is the world’s largest multilateral fund for the environment. Its family of funds work together to address the planet’s most pressing challenges in an integrated way. Its financing helps developing countries address complex challenges and work towards meeting international environmental goals. Over the past three decades, the GEF has provided more than $27 billion in financing, primarily as grants, and mobilized another $155 billion for country-driven priority projects.

This release concerns the Outrigger Technical Assistance Facility, which makes grants. A grant from the facility is not an investment, and is not a commitment by Outrigger Impact Fund I to invest. This announcement was issued on 21 April 2026 and has not been updated since; the fund reached its first close on 28 July 2026.
